Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Sunnyvale

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

After extracting the water, our Sunnyvale crew checks the materials that sat next to the unit. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasElectric units get shut down at the breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveFind the cold water shutoff above the heater and turn it clockwise. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Insurance

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Until the claim is closed, hold on to the old heater. Photograph it along with the damage, and review your policy.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Rust at the connections above the tank, either the flex lines or the inlet and outlet fittings.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve that opens or fails and discharges water.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.

What the Water Damages

Items Stored Near the Heater

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Adjacent Room Flooring

Carpet, hardwood or laminate in the hallway gets wet as water escapes below the closet door. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Shared Garage Wall

The wall between the garage and the house takes water into its bottom plate and drywall from the slab, and from there it reaches the floors of the living space.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.
