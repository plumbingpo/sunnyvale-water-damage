Attic Water Damage Help in Sunnyvale

Water reaches an attic from two directions. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in Sunnyvale inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Rusty nail tips poking through the sheathing, sometimes with water beads on them
  • Water dripping from, or staining around, a ceiling light or bathroom fan
  • Paint that peels or bubbles on the ceiling of an upstairs room
  • A musty smell in the attic or in the upstairs closets

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Air Conditioner Drips

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. For weeks an overflowing pan or blocked drain can drip into insulation, and there is no storm to tip you off.

Insulation That Stops Working

Insulation that is damp and compressed barely insulates, and drying may not bring it back. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

A Falling Ceiling

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. Wet from above, drywall loses strength at its fasteners and may fall without warning, with the wet insulation coming down too.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Attic Water Damage

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

We bag and remove insulation that is moldy or saturated, which lets the drywall and wood beneath it dry. The access path is kept contained and the living area underneath is protected.

3

Dry the Wood

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Correct the Source

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. Once the attic stays dry, new insulation goes in to restore the R-value.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Can Start

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. The sheathing, along with the top face of the drywall ceiling, is where growth can begin.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.
