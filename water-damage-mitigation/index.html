Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. It is not the repair. Mitigation covers the steps taken ahead of repairs so undamaged materials stay that way. Our Sunnyvale crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

Many homeowners treat restoration and mitigation as one and the same. In fact there are two separate stages. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurance companies also bill the two as separate line items, which makes it useful to know your current phase.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Extraction, so standing water is gone. Third is containment, where controlled airflow and plastic barriers keep contaminants and moisture away from clean rooms. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Is removing the cabinet toe kick necessary? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

Good mitigation also protects your claim. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. Arrival photos, daily moisture readings and an inventory of removed materials all go into our records. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. The repair contractor gets a clear place to start as well, without hidden moisture waiting behind fresh drywall.

How to Tell You Need Water Damage Mitigation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A roof or window opening is letting rain into the building.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • A plumber fixed the pipe but nobody has dealt with the water.

How Our Water Damage Mitigation Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Set Up Containment

Plastic barriers and controlled airflow separate wet rooms from dry ones. As a result, contaminants, dust and humid air stay put instead of spreading around the house mid-job.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. Each visit includes logged moisture readings, and the structure is usually ready for repair work within 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have an automatic shutoff fitted, so the main closes itself once a leak is sensed.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
