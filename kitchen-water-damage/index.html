Kitchen Water Damage in Sunnyvale

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. We help Sunnyvale homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

With the toe kicks off, air gets pushed below the cabinets and behind them. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

Whatever cannot be saved, whether subfloor, flooring or part of a cabinet, comes out with care. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Flooring Over Flooring

Kitchens often have new flooring laid over old. Between those layers water is trapped, unable to evaporate and invisible from above.

Sink Drain Leaks

A loose drain fitting or disposal seal leaks only while the sink is draining, so it is easy to miss. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.
