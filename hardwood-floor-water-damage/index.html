Hardwood Floor Water Damage in Sunnyvale

Wood floors react to water quickly. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

Our tool for hardwood is the floor drying mat. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Warning Signs

  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • New squeaks or popping sounds when you walk across the wet area
  • Baseboards or door trim swelling where the floor meets the wall
  • Gaps opening between boards after a floor has dried too fast
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Goes Under

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Cupping Starts

Boards swell and the edges rise. You might notice haze in the topcoat. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Swelling builds pressure across the floor. Boards crush against each other, fasteners loosen and sections can begin to lift off the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. Drying is usually no longer enough, and the bulk of the wet flooring has to be replaced.

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Refinishing Before It Is Dry

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. After the moisture leaves, each board is left with a raised center, which is crowning.

Water That Keeps Moving

Water under a floor travels. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Growth Beneath the Floor

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Rusting Fasteners

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

What We Do

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Take Baseline Readings

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Set Drying Mats

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

We track readings every day or two until boards and subfloor match dry reference areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
